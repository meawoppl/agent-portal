The evidence survives the joke.

Actual workbench recordings made for this exhibit on 9 October 2026. These are small demonstration projects, not a completed replacement robot. Recorded results (JSON).

01 · yapCAD: revise the mechanism

The agent builds a one-piece inspection fixture, orbits it, and rebuilds with a jaw opening of 34 mm instead of 20 mm: enough for a 30 mm specimen with 2 mm clearance on each side. Both recorded meshes have one body, 2,332 triangles, and are watertight. It is a fixture, not an actuated gripper. This run uses the mesh backend.

Parametric DSL source · Build command: yapcad build --file gripper.dsl --command GRIPPER --params '{"jaw_gap":34.0}'.

02 · Visilog: inspect the logic

The actual counter testbench steps through clock edges and stops on a count breakpoint before running to completion. The native run finishes at 500 ns with zero assertion failures. Waveform values use the viewer's display radix. Functional simulation is not timing sign-off.

Counter source · Testbench source.

03 · Kicadmium: route a real board

A public three-component LED fixture goes from placed components to native Kicadmium routing, then a real KiCad 9.0.9 GLB export in the workbench's 3D viewer. The router produces 13 segments, connects 3/3 nets and uses no vias. The camera motion is a recording of the actual viewer.

The first attempt with automatic copper pouring produced a zone KiCad 9 could not load. The agent reran without automatic pouring. The recorded final board loads and exports successfully. Native DRC reports no unconnected items and three footprint-library mismatch warnings. Schematic parity and ERC are unavailable for this board-only fixture. Quality inspection reports zero errors, three warnings and two informational findings. These results do not establish fabrication readiness.
